Funding the First Step from
Need to Innovation

Healthcare Innovation and Ideation Program

The Rooma and Ajay Dubey Healthcare Innovation and Ideation Program enables IIT Kanpur students to identify unmet clinical needs and develop early healthcare prototypes with mentorship and funding.

Established through a ₹2 crore contribution from IIT Kanpur alumnus Ajay Dubey and his wife, Rooma Dubey, the Healthcare Innovation and Ideation Program helps students encounter real clinical needs firsthand—then supports them with mentorship, technology and funding to begin developing solutions.

Solving the right problem first

A healthcare device may be technically elegant yet difficult for a clinician to use, too expensive for the intended setting or poorly suited to how care is actually delivered.

The central challenge is therefore not simply to invent. It is to understand the right problem before committing to a solution.

The Rooma and Ajay Dubey Healthcare Innovation and Ideation Program, or HII, was created to strengthen this crucial first step. Students observe clinical environments, identify unmet needs and translate those observations into potential health technologies.

A gift designed to seed innovation

Ajay Dubey, a 1980 graduate of IIT Kanpur’s Department of Chemical Engineering, built a four-decade career in information technology, including leadership roles at Infosys and Persistent Systems. His experience also extends to emerging technology and early-stage enterprise.

Together, Rooma and Ajay Dubey established HII to build a stronger ecosystem for student-led HealthTech innovation at IIT Kanpur.

Their philanthropy supports the stage before a start-up exists, before a product is validated and sometimes before the problem itself is fully understood.

Entering the clinical environment

Clinical immersion lies at the centre of the programme.

Before entering hospitals, fellows are trained to observe workflows systematically rather than immediately jump towards solutions. In June 2024, selected teams undertook clinical immersion at the Sree Chitra Tirunal Institute for Medical Sciences and Technology in Thiruvananthapuram.

There, problems emerged through direct observation—how clinicians monitor patients, where existing devices create difficulty and where cost or complexity limits access.

The clinical context enters the process before the prototype does.

Turning observations into prototypes

One team, comprising Harsh Arora and Sheersha Karmakar under Prof. Arjun Ramakrishnan, is developing an affordable device to monitor sedation and brain oxygenation during medical procedures.

A second team—Akshat Chouksey, Lakshit Sharma and C. Zoliansangi, mentored by Prof. Amitabha Bandyopadhyay—is developing an assistive bowel-management device for patients with neurological conditions, mobility limitations or long-term care needs.

Both projects remain at the prototyping stage. HII does not presume that every early idea will become a commercial product. It creates a stronger process for identifying which ideas deserve to advance.

Philanthropy at the earliest point of possibility

Under IIT Kanpur’s Startup Incubation and Innovation Centre, HII combines clinical exposure, engineering mentorship, development funding and entrepreneurial support.

Rooma and Ajay Dubey’s contribution does not purchase a predetermined product. It supports a repeatable innovation process—one cohort observing, designing and prototyping, while its lessons strengthen the next.

The programme teaches students to begin not with “What can I build?” but with “What does the patient need?” Because in healthcare, the first breakthrough is often not the device. It is learning to see the problem clearly.
