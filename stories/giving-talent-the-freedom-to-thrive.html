Giving Talent the Freedom
to Thrive

Batch of 1973 MCM Scholarship

Merit-cum-means, memorial and foundation-supported scholarships help IIT Kanpur students meet educational expenses and focus more fully on learning and future aspirations.

Four students. Four scholarships. Four different journeys to IIT Kanpur. Together, they show what becomes possible when financial uncertainty no longer determines how fully a student can learn, participate and imagine the future.

Merit may open the door

Every student entering IIT Kanpur has demonstrated academic ability and persistence. Yet they do not arrive with equal financial security.

For students from farming households, families facing illness or bereavement, or homes sustained by a single parent or elderly guardian, financial pressure can shape academic choices and campus participation.

Scholarships do not create talent or ambition. They create room—to focus, recover from setbacks and consider a future beyond the next expense.

The freedom to choose

Devdhar U. Patil, an Economic Sciences student from an agricultural family in Karnataka, received the Class of 1973 Merit Cum Means Scholarship.

Created through collective batch philanthropy, it reduced the financial consequences attached to academic transitions and his decision to pursue a demanding double major.

“This scholarship allowed me to focus on learning, not survival.”

Support when it mattered most

Debasis Mandal grew up in a farming household in West Bengal, where medical emergencies repeatedly placed his education at risk.

The Rajendra Rathore Memorial Scholarship helped him purchase essential academic materials and continue his MSc in Chemistry. Debasis completed the programme with exceptional performance and is now a scientist at the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre.

His journey demonstrates how memorial philanthropy can carry a life forward through another person’s opportunity.

Stability while the future is built

Tamanna Kumari Mahato, a Mechanical Engineering student from Bihar, prepared for IIT Kanpur despite limited resources, frequent relocations and personal loss.

Supported by a single elderly guardian, she continued to face financial uncertainty after arriving on campus. The Yuva Scholarship helped meet academic and living expenses and gave her greater independence.

“This support gave me freedom from constant worry.”

Replacing fear with possibility

After losing his father, Uday Singh Meena balanced education with agricultural and family responsibilities in rural Rajasthan.

The Sudhir Singhal Scholarship, supported by the Shraman Foundation, gave him the stability to remain focused on Chemistry. “This scholarship replaced fear with stability.”

He is now preparing for the Civil Services Examination, hoping to contribute to rural development and public service.

Four expressions of giving

The scholarships represent different philanthropic traditions: collective alumni giving, memorial giving, support at a vulnerable stage and organised foundation philanthropy.

Their shared belief is simple: a student who has earned a place at IIT Kanpur should be able to use that opportunity fully. A scholarship may formally fund fees, books or living costs. In practice, it can fund confidence, participation, recovery and the freedom to choose a more ambitious path.

Admission established these students’ merit. Scholarships gave that merit room to develop.

Because talent should determine how far a student can go. Financial circumstances should not.
